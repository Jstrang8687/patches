Patches logo ideas

Six directions to react to. These are rough starting points, not final art. Each one is shown on light and dark backgrounds, and at the tiny size used for browser tabs and Etsy shop icons. Pick favorites, mix pieces together, or toss them all if your brother has his own design.

Idea 1

Quilt Squares

Four sewn patches in the site's colors, one with a camera aperture and one with tree rings. It builds on the icon the site uses now, and it's easy to print on stickers, tags and boxes.

Idea 2

Sewn-On Patch

Plays straight on the name: a stitched outdoor patch, like a merit badge or trail patch, with faint mountains. It could become a real embroidered patch for hats, jackets and aprons.

Idea 3

Lens & Grain

One circle stitched together from two halves: tree growth rings on the left, a camera aperture on the right. It tells both crafts in a single mark.

Idea 4

Patchwork Fox

A fox face pieced together from patches, nodding to the wildlife photography. It's friendly and memorable, and it works as a mascot. You could swap in a different animal he photographs often.

Idea 5

Maker's Stamp

A one-color round stamp, made to be practical. It can be turned into a branding iron to burn into cutting boards, wands and tables, and used as a signature stamp on the back of prints.

Idea 6

Patch the Pup

A dog with a patch over one eye. If "Patches" is named after a real pet, this makes the story part of the brand. It could be redrawn from a photo of the actual animal.

How to use this

  • These are quick sketches to find a direction. Once you pick one, it's worth having it cleaned up, either by your brother or by a designer.
  • A good brand usually needs two versions: a full logo (like the badge) and a tiny icon (like the quilt or the stamp's "P").
  • Think about where it'll appear most: the Etsy shop icon, branding on the wood, and stickers on print packaging.
